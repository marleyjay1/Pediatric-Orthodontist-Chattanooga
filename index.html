What Does a Pediatric Orthodontist Do?

A pediatric orthodontist focuses on identifying and correcting problems involving tooth alignment, jaw development, and bite function in children. While general dentists provide routine dental care, orthodontists receive specialized training in diagnosing and treating orthodontic conditions.

Children may benefit from an orthodontic evaluation if they have:

  • Crooked or crowded teeth.
  • Gaps between their teeth.
  • Overbites, underbites, or crossbites.
  • Difficulty biting or chewing comfortably.
  • Teeth that appear out of position.
  • Early or late loss of baby teeth that may affect permanent teeth.

Visiting a Pediatric Orthodontist Chattanooga allows parents to discuss these concerns and learn whether their child needs treatment now or simply requires periodic monitoring as their smile develops.

Why Is Early Orthodontic Evaluation Important?

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends that children receive their first orthodontic checkup by age seven. At this stage, children usually have a combination of baby teeth and permanent teeth, allowing an orthodontist to evaluate developing alignment and jaw growth.

An early examination does not mean every child needs braces immediately. In many cases, the orthodontist may recommend monitoring the teeth until the child is older. However, identifying certain problems early can make it easier to plan appropriate treatment.

Early orthodontic evaluation may help identify jaw growth concerns, severe crowding, crossbites, and issues with the eruption of permanent teeth. Parents can then understand which concerns require attention and which can be observed over time.

Common Orthodontic Treatments for Children

Every child has different dental needs. A qualified orthodontist evaluates tooth position, jaw development, age, and overall oral health before recommending a suitable treatment plan.

Traditional Braces

Traditional metal braces are commonly used to correct crooked teeth, crowded teeth, gaps, and bite problems. Brackets and wires gradually guide teeth into improved positions over time. They can be an effective option for children who need comprehensive orthodontic correction.

Ceramic Braces

Ceramic braces work similarly to traditional braces but use tooth-colored or less noticeable brackets. Depending on the child's age and orthodontic needs, they may be an option for families interested in a more discreet treatment.

Clear Aligners

Clear aligners are removable trays designed to move teeth gradually. They may be suitable for some older children and teenagers, depending on their dental development, treatment complexity, and ability to wear the aligners as instructed.

Signs Your Child May Need an Orthodontic Checkup

Parents often notice early signs that their child could benefit from an orthodontic assessment. These may include difficulty chewing, visibly crowded teeth, an uneven bite, protruding front teeth, or jaws that appear misaligned.

Other signs include persistent mouth breathing, teeth that do not meet properly when biting, and baby teeth that fall out much earlier or later than expected. These symptoms do not automatically mean orthodontic treatment is necessary, but they can be useful reasons to seek professional advice.

How to Prepare Your Child for an Orthodontic Appointment

A positive first visit can help children feel more comfortable about orthodontic care. Explain that the appointment is an opportunity to check how their teeth are growing and learn how to keep their smile healthy.

Before the visit, make a note of any concerns you have noticed, including chewing difficulties, tooth crowding, or changes in your child's bite. Bring relevant dental records if available, and ask questions about treatment choices and future appointments.

Encouraging your child to participate in the conversation can also help them feel involved in their oral health decisions.
